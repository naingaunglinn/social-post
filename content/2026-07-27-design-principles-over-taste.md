## 1. Topic
The five principles Night Ace designs by — why taste debates never end and principles end them.
**Craft lane** (developers, designers, technical founders) · Pillar 4, process/behind-the-scenes.

## 2. Carousel script (8 slides)

| # | Title | Body |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Taste starts arguments. Principles end them. | *(sub: The five rules Night Ace designs by)* |
| 2 | Why principles | Debates about how something looks can run forever. Debates about a rule end quickly. A principle is a design decision you make once — then reuse on every screen that follows. |
| 3 | One job per screen | Every screen gets one job, and the layout should make it obvious. When everything is bold, nothing is. |
| 4 | Whitespace is structure | Empty space isn't wasted space — it's how readers know what belongs together. Crowded layouts push that work onto the visitor. |
| 5 | Type does the heavy lifting | Most of an interface is text. Get the type right — size, spacing, contrast — and the design is mostly finished before a single decoration appears. |
| 6 | Consistency beats novelty | The second time a pattern appears, it should look exactly like the first. People learn an interface once; don't make them relearn it on every page. |
| 7 | Everything earns its place | If removing an element changes nothing, it was decoration. Remove things until something breaks, then put only that one thing back. |
| 8 | Designed on purpose | Principles are why a design survives contact with real content. **CTA:** Want a site designed on purpose? Send Night Ace a message — we'll show you our reasoning, not just our portfolio. |

## 4. LinkedIn caption
Taste starts arguments. Principles end them.

Every studio says it has good taste. Almost none can tell you their rules. That gap is where projects stall — endless revisions, "make it pop," decisions that reopen every week.

We keep our principles short enough to hold in your head:

— Every screen gets one job
— Whitespace is structure, not leftovers
— Type does the heavy lifting
— Consistency beats novelty
— Everything on the page earns its place

None of these are original. What matters is that they're written down, so design debates become short conversations about rules instead of long conversations about feelings.

What's a design rule you refuse to break? Genuinely curious what other builders hold onto.
